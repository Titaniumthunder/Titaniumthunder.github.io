Folded, Quantized Systolic MAC Array Accelerator

In Progress
Sep 2026 – Apr 2027 · Verilog · Python / NumPy · Icarus Verilog · GTKWave · Vivado · OpenLane · SkyWater sky130

A small AI accelerator chip: a systolic array of multiply-accumulate (MAC) units running quantized matrix multiplication, the same core operation behind GPU tensor cores. Two choices keep it buildable in two semesters: a folded array that reuses a small physical grid across cycles to act like a larger one, and INT8 / INT4 quantization instead of floating point.

Research questionHow do fold factor and operand bit-width trade off against chip area, power, timing, and accuracy?

  • I own the RTL: the processing-element datapath (each PE computes psum += a × w every cycle; activations flow across rows, partial sums flow down columns) and the folding-controller FSM that steps a small physical array through a larger logical one.
  • Built to be swept: INT8 baseline plus an INT4 variant, with fold factor and bit-width as RTL parameters, so one codebase produces every design point in the study.
  • Full flow, as a team: checked against a Python golden model, brought up on a Xilinx FPGA, then carried through OpenLane RTL-to-GDSII with DRC, LVS, and timing signoff. Teammates: James (verification, FPGA bring-up) and Marco (physical design, signoff).

RF Jamming-Detection CubeSat

Complete · RF link verified
May – Jul 2026 · C · Salvo RTOS · dsPIC33 · I²C · AX.25 · GNU Radio · USRP SDR

Satellite-side firmware for an RF jamming-detection CubeSat. The mission: a Raspberry Pi payload detects jamming attacks, and the satellite downlinks the evidence (attack type, frequency, and GPS location) to a ground station. I owned the full communications pipeline between the Pi payload, the dsPIC33 flight computer, and the EnduroSat UHF transceiver, building on a previous researcher's codebase.

  • Verified end-to-end downlink: three telemetry types (LOG, TEL, IMG), including jamming-detection JSON carrying attack type, frequency, and GPS fix, travel from the Pi over I²C to the OBC and transmit via AX.25 at 435 MHz (9.6 kbaud FSK) to a GNU Radio ground station on a USRP N2920, with CRC-32-validated beacons per the EnduroSat ESTTC protocol.
  • Closed the loop over RF: validated the on-demand command path end to end. A ground-station uplink command reaches the flight computer, which polls the Pi payload over I²C and downlinks the jamming report back to the ground station.
  • Concurrency design: multiple RTOS tasks (telemetry beacon, payload polling, uplink listener) share one I²C bus with a half-duplex transceiver. My binary-semaphore arbitration eliminated recurring system freezes, with zero recurrence across all subsequent FlatSat runs. Root cause: the semaphore was being released between 16-byte transfer chunks, letting other tasks corrupt messages mid-stream.
  • Cross-stack debugging: isolated three independent faults via single-variable testing: a shared-buffer race condition, a missing carriage-return terminator against the ESTTC protocol spec, and a blocking GPS serial read starving the main loop (fixed with a background daemon thread and cached fix).
  • Byte-survival study: assigned by Dr. Vejarano, confirmed a 97-byte payload ceiling and proved 0x00 truncation through the I²C C-string interface, establishing mandatory hex-encoding for image data to survive the full Pi → OBC → RF path.
  • Handoff documentation: wrote a 20-page technical manual (Rev 1.1) covering the full comms stack, PDU captures, and the semaphore and timing design, so the next researcher starts from a working baseline. Also submitted a SURP research abstract.
